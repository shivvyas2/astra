Appendix A

AWhy Astrya exists

Most astrology apps either guess the sky or hand a language model a sun sign and let it write. Astrya computes the chart with the Swiss Ephemeris and lets Claude interpret only what was computed. Everything else in this book follows from that one split.

This appendix is the product story behind the code on main on 30 September 2026, version 0.3.0. It is written the way a launch document would be: the problem, the bet, who the app is for, what each release delivered, and a press release with the questions a sceptical reader would ask. Where it makes a claim about behaviour, the claim is grounded in a file you can open. The design notes under docs/design/ record intent; where they and the code disagree, this appendix follows the code and says so.

A.1The problem

An astrology reading has two parts: the chart, and what the chart means. The first part is astronomy. Where the Sun, Moon and planets stood at a particular minute over a particular town is a fact, and it can be computed to a fraction of a degree. The second part is interpretation, which is a craft with a vocabulary of its own: lords, aspects, dignity, periods, transits. A good reading rests the second part on the first.

Consumer apps mostly fail at one end or the other. Some never compute a chart at all: they know a birth date, look up a sun sign, and serve a paragraph that is true of a twelfth of the population. Others compute a chart and then hand a language model a loose description of it and hope. The model, given sign-level facts, writes sign-level prose. The specific-readings design note from 17 September 2026 puts the failure precisely: "A model given sign-level facts writes sign-level prose; the instructions have said 'never a generic horoscope' all along and cannot fix an input problem."

There is a third failure the first design note names directly: a model asked to state a planetary position will state one, whether or not it is true. A language model has no ephemeris. Left to itself it will put Saturn wherever the sentence wants Saturn to be.

A.2The bet

The first design note, dated 23 July 2026, states the bet in one line: "Claude never guesses planetary positions. It interprets computed values." The code keeps that promise with a wall between two kinds of work. On one side, deterministic computation from the birth moment. On the other, a model that is handed the results and told, in the system prompt itself, "These are the only facts you have."

The computed side is computeChart in lib/astrology/chart.ts. It turns a local birth time into universal time with luxon, asks the ephemeris for a Julian day, sets Lahiri as the ayanamsa for Vedic and leaves Western tropical, computes Placidus cusps and the ascendant with houses_ex, then places eight bodies with calc_ut and derives Ketu as Rahu plus 180 degrees. For Vedic it adds nakshatras, the current Vimshottari period, and, since 0.3.0, a derived block: what each planet rules, what it aspects, its dignity, whether it is combust, and where the running dasha lord sits. Both traditions are computed once at intake and stored in birth_profiles.chart.

The interpreted side is buildChartSystem. It renders the chart as text, then sets the rules. The two that carry the product are these, quoted from the prompt: "Never state a placement, lordship, aspect, dasha, condition or number that is not listed above," and "No sign-personality writing. A sentence that would be true of a twelfth of the population is not an answer." The same wall holds outside chat. The daily readings and dosha alerts in lib/alerts/ decide in code whether a dosha exists, and the model's only job, as the comment in compose.ts says, is "to say what the change means and what to do about it." On the phone, the on-device model may only restate rows from a table it looked up.

Who it is for

The app is for someone who wants a reading of their own chart rather than their sign, and who is willing to type a birth time and a birthplace to get it. Two traditions are served, chosen per conversation: Vedic, sidereal with Lahiri, printed as a kundli because, as the PDF renderer's comment puts it, "in most families it is a piece of paper that gets kept"; and Western, tropical. Numerology is a third mode with its own prompt. The choices in the code say who the author had in mind: the cron schedule is annotated in IST, the kundli PDF prints Sanskrit rashi names because "a North Indian chart labelled Scorpio reads as a translation of someone else's diagram," and the alerts vocabulary is Sade Sati, Mangal dosha and Kaal Sarp. The Western mode is real but plainer, and A.5 says why.

0.1.0: the whole product, once

The first tag is large because it is the first tag. The changelog's Added list is the feature list of the README: the chart engine for both traditions, streaming readings with an optional deep reading on Opus, the kundli PDF, the life timeline with moments pinned by hand or proposed from chat history "and confirmed before anything is saved," morning and night readings on the user's local clock with a push only when a dosha or hard transit starts or ends, the native app, Siri and widgets, the admin area and the landing page. Two entries sit under Changed. One is the rename, covered below. The other is a correctness fix in the dasha engine: antardashas are laid out from each mahadasha's "virtual start" rather than from the birth moment, which is what the virtualStart field in lib/astrology/dasha.ts records, so that sub-periods line up with the real period.

0.2.0: the timeline learns to speak

Tagged the same day. The timeline had drawn the dasha periods but named them only by planet. This release adds a one-line theme and a short meaning per period, written by Claude from the chart and the moments pinned inside it, stored in period_readings with a hash of those moments so a changed period is rewritten and an unchanged one is not. The chat gains the same timeline as a block in its cached prompt, so "why was 2021 so hard" is answered against the user's 2021. The scan now resolves relative dates and ages from the message date and birth date, quotes the words each moment came from, and keeps undated events for the user to date. A Home Screen and Lock Screen widget shows the current period. The one fix matters operationally: the app had reported its push environment from the build configuration rather than the signing entitlement, so a Release build from Xcode registered a sandbox token as production and never received a push.

0.3.0: readings cite the chart, not the sign

This is the release that closes the gap named in A.1. Every Vedic reading now carries the lord of each house and where that lord sits, which planets aspect what, each planet's dignity, which are combust, and where the running dasha lord is placed. Transits are read as gochara, by the house they occupy from the ascendant and from the natal Moon, with the affliction those positions raise. Numerology sees the personal year and month, the Lo Shu grid, and the relationship between the core numbers. The phone answers questions about rulers, aspects and strength on the device.

The Changed entry is the one users could notice: Vedic houses became whole-sign, because the chart, the PDF and the on-device table had labelled houses whole-sign from the ascendant while filling them with Placidus numbers. "Existing charts upgrade themselves the first time they are read; nothing to do." The Fixed list is the consequence of doing that carefully. Western readings had been handed Vedic technique on a tropical chart and now get "the plain sky again." And a dosha fixed at birth could have announced itself as news when the house system shifted the detectors; "Natal conditions never alert now; they reconcile silently." Both of those are decisions in their own right, and Appendix B records them.

The name

The product was called Astra from the first commit on 23 July 2026 until the commit of 16 September 2026 that renamed the iOS target, the copy and the prompts. The changelog for 0.1.0 states the rule: "The bundle identifier and production hostname are unchanged." So the repository is still astra, the bundle id is com.shivvyas.astra, the App Group is group.com.shivvyas.astra, the production host is astra.shivvyas.com, and the first migration is headed "Astra schema + RLS." The user-facing name is Astrya everywhere: the system prompt opens "You are Astrya," the widget bundle is SancharaWidgets, and the custom URL scheme a widget tap uses is sanchara://. Why the identifiers stayed is a decision in Appendix B; in short, Apple's push, sign-in and associated-domain configuration is wired to them.

A.4PR/FAQ

Press release

Astrya computes your real birth chart and reads it to you. Give it the date, time and place you were born and the Swiss Ephemeris places every planet, house, nakshatra and period exactly. Then ask it anything. The reading names the placement it is reading from, in the Vedic or Western tradition you choose, and it will not tell you something that is true of everyone born under your sign. Twice a day it writes a reading for your morning and your night, and it only sends a notification when something in your sky actually changes. On iPhone the chart lives on the phone, so Siri, the widget and simple questions answer with no network and no charge. Your kundli is a document you can print.

Does the model compute my chart?

No. computeChart in lib/astrology/chart.ts does, using swisseph-wasm inside the route handler, and the result is stored with your profile. The model is handed the result as text and told it is "the only facts you have." If you ask about a placement that is not in the chart, the prompt instructs it to say what the chart does show instead.

Why is my Vedic chart different from my Western one?

Two settings, both in computeChart. Vedic is computed sidereal with the Lahiri ayanamsa, which currently sets positions roughly 24 degrees behind tropical, so a Sun in early Capricorn tropically is in Sagittarius sidereally; the test in chart.test.ts asserts exactly that. Vedic houses are whole-sign from the ascendant; Western houses are Placidus.

Why did my house numbers change in September?

Before 0.3.0 the Vedic chart labelled its houses whole-sign but numbered the planets by Placidus cusps, so a planet could be drawn in a house whose sign it was not in. Since 0.3.0 both agree. A stored chart from before the change carries schemaVersion below 2 and is recomputed the first time it is read, by ensureCurrentChart in lib/data/birthProfile.ts.

Will I get a notification every day?

Two readings are written every day, morning and night on your own clock, and they arrive as pushes when push is configured. A dosha alert is different: it is sent only when a condition starts or ends. The daily job diffs the conditions it computes today against the open rows in transit_conditions, and a condition that merely continues is never re-notified, "which is what keeps a standing Sade Sati from becoming a daily nag." A condition fixed at birth is never announced as news at all.

What does a reading cost to run?

Measured on a real chart and question, and recorded in docs/MODEL_COSTS.md: a mid-conversation turn on Sonnet 5 went from roughly $0.023 to $0.008 after the system prompt was split so its stable half is a cache read, and after routine readings were set to low effort, which cut output from 1,452 to 719 tokens for the same placements cited. A deep reading keeps the default effort on Opus. On an iPhone with Apple Intelligence, a lookup question never reaches the API at all.

Why is a Western reading plainer than a Vedic one?

Because the techniques that make a Vedic reading specific are Vedic. Lordship, graha drishti, moolatrikona, combustion orbs and the natal doshas apply to a sidereal whole-sign chart, and applying them to a tropical Placidus chart would cite Indian technique as though it were Western. So factsFor returns nothing for a Western chart and the prompt renders the plain positions; today's sky is given as where each body is and which natal body it sits on.

Who can read my conversations?

You, through Row Level Security policies that scope every table to auth.uid(), and an admin who signs in at /admin/login and appears in the admins table, which has no insert policy, so signing up can never grant it. The admin area reads through the service-role client on the server and is read-only for transcripts.

Check yourself

What is the one thing the model is never allowed to do, and where is it enforced?

State a fact that was not computed. It is enforced by construction (the model has no ephemeris and only sees text the engine wrote) and by instruction (the Accuracy block in buildChartSystem, and the alert and prediction prompts' "Never invent a placement, dosha, date, or number that is not listed above").

Why does a Western reading get no derived facts?

Because every technique in derived.ts is Vedic and assumes whole-sign houses from a sidereal ascendant. factsFor returns undefined unless chart.tradition is "vedic".

Which identifiers still say Astra, and why were they kept?

The bundle id, the App Group, the URL name, the production host and the repository. Apple's push credentials, Sign in with Apple and associated domains are configured against them; changing them would mean new App IDs and re-setup of every capability.

What did 0.2.0 fix that no user could have diagnosed?

The push environment was read from the build configuration rather than the signing entitlement, so a Release build run from Xcode registered a sandbox token as production and silently never received a push.
