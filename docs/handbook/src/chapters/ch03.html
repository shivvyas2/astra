Chapter three

3Readings, prompts, and cost

Claude never computes a chart and never sees one it was not handed. A reading is an interpretation of numbers the Swiss Ephemeris produced, delivered through a system prompt built in two halves so that the expensive half is paid for once and read from cache after that.

This chapter describes the reading pipeline as it stands on main on 30 September 2026, version 0.3.0. It covers the prompt builders in lib/astrology/prompt.ts, the model and effort settings in lib/anthropic.ts, the caching and history rules that the chat route applies, the one test file whose name promises more than it delivers, and the measured cost figures in docs/MODEL_COSTS.md. The chat feature that sits on top of all this, with its route handler, tables and clients, is chapter 6. The chart engine that produces the facts is chapter 2.

3.1What a reading is

A reading is one streamed answer from Claude to one question, grounded in facts the server computed. The facts are the user's stored chart (birth_profiles.chart, holding a Vedic and a Western chart side by side), the derived facts attached to the Vedic one (lords, aspects, dignity, combustion, natal doshas), the numerology of the birth date and name, today's sky read against the natal chart, and the pinned moments of the user's life timeline. Claude is told, in so many words, that these are the only facts it has.

Three modes exist, and the type system keeps two of them apart from the third. Tradition in lib/astrology/types.ts is "vedic" | "western": the two ways of computing a chart. ChatMode adds "numerology", which has no chart at all and reads from the birth date and the name. The chat route accepts a ChatMode; only the two chart modes ever reach the ephemeris.

A reading is also either standard or deep. Standard runs on Sonnet with brief thinking and a 160 word length rule; deep runs on Opus with the default thinking budget and 320 words. The client decides with one boolean, deep, in the request body.

Chat is not the only caller. Every model call in the repository goes through the one client in lib/anthropic.ts, and three other places make readings of their own: the daily morning and night readings, the dosha alerts, and the timeline's period meanings and moment extraction. They share the prompt builders and the effort setting, which is why this chapter is a foundations chapter and not a chat chapter.

3.3The system prompt in two halves

The system prompt is two strings, built by two functions, sent as two blocks in the system array of the request. The first block is everything that never changes for a user. The second is everything that does. The split exists for one reason, stated in the comment above buildChartSystem: the first half carries a cache breakpoint, so it must be byte-identical on every turn and across conversations, and anything that varies has to live in the second half and come after it.

The stable half: buildChartSystem

buildChartSystem takes the first name, the tradition, the chart, optionally the derived facts, and optionally the two numerology numbers. It returns one string in a fixed order: an identity line, the facts block, the numerology line, then three rule lists headed Accuracy, How to answer and Format, and a closing line. The identity line names the system the way the astrologer would: "Vedic (sidereal, Lahiri)" or "Western (tropical)". The facts block is introduced as the user's real chart, computed with the Swiss Ephemeris, and the sentence that follows is the whole grounding contract: these are the only facts you have.

The Accuracy rules forbid stating any placement, lordship, aspect, dasha, condition or number not listed above; require every claim to name the placement it reads from, its house and the technique; ask for tendencies and timing rather than guaranteed outcomes; and tell the model to say what the chart does show when it does not show what was asked, or to ask one short question when one missing detail would change the answer. The How to answer rules ban sign-personality writing outright: a sentence that would be true of a twelfth of the population is not an answer, and the model is told to delete it and read a house lord, an aspect, or the dasha lord's placement instead. The Format rules ask for two or three short sections under bold markdown headings, plain text with no emoji and no bold inside sentences, and a final section titled In simple words. The last line is the one the tests check for the spirit of the thing: never invent, and never flatter, and if the honest reading is unremarkable, say so plainly.

The facts block has two renderers, and which one runs is the Vedic versus Western branch of the whole system.

The Vedic branch: renderChartFacts

When derived is present the block is rendered by renderChartFacts, which writes five labelled sections from the Derived structure that lib/astrology/derived.ts computes:

  • The ascendant, marked as setting house 1, and the Sun and Moon signs.
  • PLACEMENTS: one line per planet from planetLine. Each line gives sign, degree, house and nakshatra, then the houses the planet rules, its dignity word (exalted, debilitated, in moolatrikona, in its own sign, neutral), "within a degree of exact" when it sits under a degree from its deep point, combustion with the distance from the Sun, retrograde, the houses it aspects, and the planets it is conjunct with.
  • HOUSES: one line per house giving its sign, its lord, where that lord sits and with what dignity, which planets occupy the house and which aspect it.
  • CURRENT PERIOD: the mahadasha and antardasha lords with their dates and, for each, the lord's own natal placement, dignity and rulerships, because that placement is what gives the period its character.
  • STANDING CONDITIONS IN THE BIRTH CHART: the natal doshas, one label and one detail line each.

Two grammar helpers exist because the model copies the prompt's register. houseSuffix returns "s" for a plural list of houses and nothing for one, so "aspects house 9" and "aspects houses 4, 7, 8" both read correctly; the test suite pins both forms. RELATIONSHIP_PHRASE phrases a number relationship with the pair as the subject ("are friends", "are enemies") to avoid an article agreement error. One sentinel matters more than the grammar: a house whose lord is not a body in the chart comes out of deriveFacts with lordHouse of 0, and the renderer says "lord Sun (not a body in this chart)" rather than printing house 0 as though it were a placement. Every real chart has all nine bodies, so this is only reachable from a hand-built fixture, but a prompt that stated a placement that does not exist would be teaching the model to do the same.

The Western branch: renderChart

When derived is absent the block is rendered by renderChart: the ascendant, the Sun and Moon signs, the dasha line if the chart has one, and one plain line per planet with sign, degree, house, nakshatra and a retrograde flag. No lordship, no aspects, no dignity, no doshas. The comment above the function explains why, and the fix it records is worth knowing because it was a real bug in this release. Every technique renderChartFacts adds is Vedic. Handing it to a Western reading would cite Indian technique and Vedic doshas as though they were tropical, and the whole-sign house numbers it derives would disagree with chart.planets[].house, which for a Western chart is Placidus, and which is what the PDF and the iOS chart view actually render.

The gate is not in the prompt module at all. factsFor in lib/astrology/derived.ts returns undefined for any chart whose tradition is not "vedic", and returns the stored derived block, or a freshly computed one for a chart written before derived facts shipped, otherwise. The chat route calls factsFor(chart) and passes whatever comes back, so the Western branch is chosen by the chart's own tradition field and no caller can opt a Western chart into Vedic technique by mistake. The transit text follows the same rule in describeGochara: houses from the ascendant and from the Moon, and the transit afflictions, are added only when the natal chart is Vedic; a Western reading gets the plain sky, where each body is and which natal body it sits over.

The numerology branch: buildNumerologySystem

Numerology has no chart, so it has its own stable builder. buildNumerologySystem introduces Sanchara as a Vedic numerologist and lists the real numbers computed from the birth date and the name: the mulank (root, from the birth day), the bhagyank (destiny, from the full date), the namank (from the full name, which is quoted in the prompt), the digits that repeat in the birth date and the digits that are missing from it (the Lo Shu grid), and whether the namank and the mulank are friends, neutral or enemies. Its Accuracy rule is stricter in one way: every number cited must match the values above exactly. Its Format rules are the same as the chart prompt's, down to the closing In simple words section.

The volatile half: buildTodaySystem

buildTodaySystem takes the day, optionally the transit text, optionally a length, and optionally the personal year and month. It writes "Today is …" with an instruction to use it for anything about today, now, or the current period; the personal cycle line when given (used by numerology); a SKY TODAY block when transits are given, followed on its own line by the instruction to name the transiting planet and the natal house or planet it touches; and the length rule, "160 words or fewer unless they ask for more" by default. The chat route sets 160 for a standard reading and 320 for a deep one; the daily readings job sets 150.

The word "today" is deliberately coarse. describeToday in lib/astrology/transits.ts renders the weekday and long date plus a part of day (morning before noon, afternoon before five, evening before nine, night after) and the zone name, in the user's own timezone. The route computes it with Luxon in profile.timezone, never the server's clock. A clock time here would change the prompt every minute, which is the first of the two things that used to break the cache.

What the route appends

The route appends one more thing to the stable string before sending it: the life timeline. describeTimelineForPrompt in lib/timeline/describe.ts renders the current dasha period (Vedic only, since Western has no dashas) and up to MAX_MOMENTS, which is 30, of the user's pinned moments newest first, each with its date at the precision it was pinned and, for Vedic, the period it fell in. It returns an empty string when there is nothing to say, so the route can append it unconditionally. It goes in the stable half on purpose, and the route's comment says why: it only changes when the user pins or removes a moment, so it belongs with the chart and not with the day.

Why byte-identical, and how it is kept so

A cached prefix is matched by exact bytes. If the stable half differed by one character between two turns, every turn would write a new cache entry and read none. So the rule is that nothing in buildChartSystem may depend on the clock, the sky, the length setting, or the order in which a JSON store returns fields. Three tests in lib/astrology/prompt.test.ts hold the line: the stable half must not contain "Today is", "SKY TODAY" or "words or fewer"; two calls with the same inputs must return the same string; and a Derived that has been through JSON.parse(JSON.stringify(…)), which is how a stored chart returns it, must render identically to a freshly computed one.

buildSystemPrompt and buildNumerologyPrompt join the two halves into one string for callers that do not cache. No route calls them today; the tests do, and they are the simplest way to see the whole prompt at once.

The blank line before the numerology sentence renderChartFacts ends whichever labelled block comes last with no trailing blank line. The numerology line therefore begins with two newlines of its own, or it would land inside HOUSES, CURRENT PERIOD or STANDING CONDITIONS and read as one more item of that block. The test named for it checks that the line before "Numerology:" is empty.

3.4Models and effort

// lib/anthropic.ts
export const READING_MODEL = "claude-sonnet-5";
export const DEEP_READING_MODEL = "claude-opus-4-8";

  return model === "claude-sonnet-5" || model === "claude-opus-4-8";
}

export const LOW_EFFORT: { readonly output_config: { readonly effort: "low" } } = {
  output_config: { effort: "low" },
};

Two models, one switch. Standard readings run on Sonnet 5, which the file's comment describes as intelligent and stable with adaptive thinking for well-reasoned interpretation. A deep reading upgrades to Opus 4.8. Both support adaptive thinking; the comment notes that Haiku 4.5 would not, which is why supportsAdaptiveThinking exists as a function rather than a constant. Every caller spreads thinking: { type: "adaptive" } into the request only when that function says yes.

LOW_EFFORT is the more consequential export. Thinking tokens are billed as output, and output is the expensive half of a reading. The comment above the constant records a measurement made on a standard Vedic reading of the same chart and question:

SettingOutput tokens
adaptive thinking, default effort1,452
adaptive thinking, effort "low"719
thinking disabled328

All three cited the same real placements. That is the finding that set the policy: routine readings run at low effort, reasoning intact and spend halved, and deep readings keep the default, because the default is what a person is asking for when they turn Deep on. The chat route applies it with one spread, ...(body.deep ? {} : LOW_EFFORT). The daily readings, the alert copy and both timeline jobs apply it unconditionally.

The type of LOW_EFFORT is written out longhand because output_config is generally available on the API but postdates the types in the installed SDK (@anthropic-ai/sdk at ^0.68.0 in package.json). The value is cast so the wire format stays exact. The same is true of "adaptive", which each caller casts through unknown to Anthropic.ThinkingConfigParam.

max_tokens is set per caller and is a ceiling, not a target. The route's comment is explicit that the length rule in the prompt is what governs how much is generated. The values are 1,600 for a standard chat reading, 3,000 for a deep one, and 1,600 for both the daily readings and the alert copy.

A stale comment in the route Above the thinking spread in app/api/chat/route.ts a comment still says that only the deep (Opus) model supports adaptive thinking and that Haiku rejects it. That was true of an earlier model pair. supportsAdaptiveThinking is the source of record, and it returns true for both current models, so standard readings think too, at low effort.

3.5Caching and the cost of a turn

The API is stateless. Every turn re-sends the system prompt and the whole retained history and is billed for all of it as input. Prompt caching lets a request declare breakpoints; the prefix up to a breakpoint that matches a recent request byte for byte is billed as a cache read at a tenth of the input rate. The chat route sets two.

The two breakpoints

The first breakpoint is on the stable system block: cache_control: { type: "ephemeral", ttl: "1h" }. The hour, per the route's comment, is so that a reply half an hour later still reads the chart from cache rather than paying full price for it again. The second is on the last stored turn of the history: the route maps the retained turns into MessageParams and gives the final one a content array with cache_control: { type: "ephemeral" }. From the second message of a conversation onward the whole prefix, chart, today and history, is a cache read.

The route's comment and docs/MODEL_COSTS.md both record the subtlety. A prefix below the API's minimum cacheable length, 1,024 tokens on Sonnet, is silently not cached. The measured stable half is 942 tokens, so on its own it may fall under the line, and the first breakpoint is then simply ignored. That is why the conversation breakpoint, not the system one, does the real work: by the second turn the prefix up to the last stored turn is well over the minimum, and it includes the system blocks.

Making the prompt hold still

Two things used to change on every request and had to stop before caching could work. The date line carried a clock time, so the prompt differed every minute; it is now the date plus a part of day, as describeToday renders it. And transits were computed for the current instant. transitChart in lib/astrology/transits.ts now computes them for midday UTC and memoises the promise in a module-level map keyed by day, tradition and the latitude and longitude rounded to whole degrees, so users in the same city share an entry. The map is cleared when it reaches MAX_ENTRIES, 200, and a failed computation is deleted from it so an error is never cached. Midday is the sample point because the Moon is the only body that moves enough within a day to matter, and sampling the middle halves the worst-case drift. The memo also stops the ephemeris running on every message, which was the lesser of its two purposes.

The history budget

// lib/data/history.ts
export const HISTORY_BUDGET_CHARS = 6000;

export function selectHistory(turns: Turn[], budget: number = HISTORY_BUDGET_CHARS): Turn[] {
  const kept: Turn[] = [];
  let used = 0;
  for (let i = turns.length - 1; i >= 0; i--) {
    const turn = turns[i];
    const cost = turn.content.length;
    if (used + cost > budget && kept.length > 0) break;
    kept.unshift(turn);
    used += cost;
  }
  while (kept.length > 0 && kept[0].role === "assistant") kept.shift();
  return kept;
}

The route loads every stored message of the conversation with getMessages in lib/data/chat.ts, ordered by created_at ascending, and hands them to selectHistory. The budget is in characters, roughly four to a token, so 6,000 characters is about 1,500 tokens. It walks newest first, because the most recent exchange matters most for coherence, and stops when the next turn would overflow, except that the newest turn is always kept even if it alone exceeds the budget. It then drops any leading assistant turns, because the API requires the first message to be from the user, and a reply with no question above it reads as a non sequitur anyway. The old rule was a flat last ten turns; the budget replaced it so that one long reading cannot crowd out the recent exchange, and so a conversation that runs all evening does not keep getting more expensive.

Watching it in production

After the stream ends the route awaits finalMessage() and logs one line per turn: 

chat usage conv=<id> model=claude-sonnet-5 in=13 cache_write=0 cache_read=2867 out=538

The fields are the API's input_tokens, cache_creation_input_tokens, cache_read_input_tokens and output_tokens. The diagnostic rule from the cost document: cache_read staying at 0 across a conversation means something reintroduced a per-request change into the prefix, and the date line and the transit text are the first two places to look.

The cheapest turn never reaches the API

docs/MODEL_COSTS.md ends with the one saving this chapter does not cover. On iOS 26 with Apple Intelligence, questions that are lookups rather than interpretation ("where is my Saturn", "which dasha am I in") are answered by the on-device model from the cached chart, and never become a paid turn at all. The saving is in the turn count, not the per-turn price. The router, the tool it reads through, and the vocabulary rules it must obey are in chapter 10.

3.6Guarding the prompt

There is no filter in this repository that inspects a user's message for instructions aimed at the model. The guard is structural, and it is worth being precise about what it consists of.

User text goes in messages, never in system. The route reads body.message, appends it to the messages array and stores it; it is never interpolated into either system string. Two kinds of user-authored text do reach the stable half, and both are the user's own data reaching only the user's own prompt: the first name (and, for numerology, the full name, which is quoted as the source of the namank), and the titles of the moments the user has pinned on their timeline, which describeTimelineForPrompt renders verbatim.

The system prompt contains only computed facts and fixed rules. The chart block is rendered from numbers the ephemeris produced. The Accuracy rules tell the model that those numbers are the only facts it has and that a sentence with no placement behind it does not go in the answer. This is what makes an invented placement unlikely: the model has a complete, labelled list, and an instruction to cite from it.

Identity is checked twice. The route calls supabase.auth.getUser() and returns 401 without a user. getOrCreateConversation in lib/data/chat.ts, when handed an existing conversation id, verifies that the row belongs to this user_id before reusing it and throws "Conversation not found" otherwise; its comment calls this defence in depth on top of RLS. The tables themselves carry owner-only policies, described in chapter 6. So a user cannot append to, or read history from, a conversation that is not theirs, and the history that becomes part of the prompt is always their own.

What lib/data/injection.test.ts actually tests

Despite its name, this file has nothing to do with prompt injection. It tests dependency injection: that the data helpers accept an injected Supabase client and use it, and fall back to the cookie client only when none is given. Its three cases are that saveBirthProfile writes through the injected client, that appendMessage writes through the injected client with exactly the row shape the messages table expects, and that appendMessage with no client calls createServerSupabase once.

Why that matters to readings is the bearer path. A web request carries Supabase cookies, and createServerSupabase in lib/supabase/server.ts builds a client from them. An iOS request carries Authorization: Bearer <jwt> and no cookies. createRouteSupabase in lib/supabase/route.ts reads that header and returns a plain supabase-js client that forwards the token, so Row Level Security evaluates as that user. Every helper in lib/data/chat.ts and lib/data/birthProfile.ts takes an optional db?: Db for this reason. If a helper ignored it and built its own cookie client, an iOS turn would run as nobody: the conversation insert would fail RLS and the reading would never be stored. The test pins that createServerSupabase is not called when a client is injected, which is the whole contract.

Name versus contents If you came to injection.test.ts looking for prompt injection guards, the honest answer is that the guards are the four structural facts above, and the tests that hold them are in prompt.test.ts (the rules text is present) and history.test.ts (the history is well formed). The chat route itself has no test file.

3.7Tests

All are Vitest, run with npm test, and none touches the network. The prompt tests build from a fixture chart (Leo ascendant, Sun in Sagittarius, Moon in Taurus, Venus in Libra, Venus mahadasha, Sun antardasha) and compute its derived facts once so every assertion reads the same numbers the prompt was rendered from.

  • lib/astrology/prompt.test.ts, buildSystemPrompt: the joined prompt carries the name, the tradition, the ascendant, a nakshatra and the dasha lord; it contains "never state a placement", "only this data", "guidance and reflection" and "in simple words"; the stable half carries no "Today is", no "SKY TODAY" and no length rule, while the volatile half carries all three; and two calls with the same inputs return the same string.
  • buildTodaySystem transit framing: with a multi-line transit block the instruction lands on its own line, not glued to a bullet, and there is no double period.
  • derived facts in the prompt: pinned to the fixture's computed values rather than the shape of a sentence. The Sun's line says what it rules; the Moon at 3.1° Taurus is exalted; Venus in house 3 "aspects house 9", singular; a Mars in Aries lagna "aspects houses 4, 7, 8"; house 10's line names Venus in house 3 in Libra as its lord; the mahadasha line says where Venus sits; the generic register ban ("true of a twelfth of the population") is present; and a lord that is not a body in the chart is said to be so rather than placed in house 0.
  • a Western chart gets no derived facts: the C1 regression. With a Leo ascendant and Mars stored in Placidus house 5, whole-sign counting would put it in house 6; the test asserts the prompt reads "Mars: Capricorn 2°, h5", contains no moolatrikona, no "aspects house" and no STANDING CONDITIONS, through both buildChartSystem with no derived and buildSystemPrompt's default.
  • numerology stays its own block: the blank line before "Numerology: Mulank 3, Bhagyank 7."
  • derived facts survive a JSON round trip: fresh and round-tripped Derived render identically.
  • numerology prompt: for the birth date 1990-07-15 the repeated digits are 1 and 9, the missing digits 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8, and "The namank 1 and the mulank 6 are enemies." appears as one literal sentence, so a swapped argument order or a wrong relationship word fails.
  • buildTodaySystem with personal cycles: distinct year and month values both render, and neither appears when absent.
  • lib/data/history.test.ts: everything is kept when it fits; the oldest turns drop first; a window never starts on an assistant turn (and can be empty as a result); the newest turn is kept even when it alone exceeds the budget; an empty conversation yields nothing.
  • lib/data/injection.test.ts: the three dependency injection cases in section 3.6, with computeChart stubbed so the ephemeris binary is not loaded.

3.8Study notes

Read in this order

  1. lib/astrology/prompt.ts, top to bottom. Read renderChart's comment before renderChartFacts, so the Western branch makes sense as the older, plainer one.
  2. lib/astrology/derived.ts, just factsFor and the comment above it, for the gate that chooses the branch.
  3. lib/astrology/transits.ts, for the memo key and describeToday, which are the two things that made the prompt hold still.
  4. lib/anthropic.ts, for the three measured effort numbers.
  5. app/api/chat/route.ts, from the profile read to the messages.stream call, watching for the two cache_control objects.
  6. lib/data/history.ts, for the budget.
  7. docs/MODEL_COSTS.md, for what each change bought.
  8. lib/astrology/prompt.test.ts, which reads as the list of rules in plain English.

Check yourself

Why is the timeline appended to the stable half rather than the today half, when it is user data that can change?

Because it changes only when the user pins or removes a moment, which is rare, while the today half changes several times a day. Putting it in the stable half means a pin costs one cache write; putting it in the today half would gain nothing, since the today half is not behind its own breakpoint.

The stable half measures 942 tokens and Sonnet's minimum cacheable prefix is 1,024. Why does caching work at all?

Because of the second breakpoint on the last stored turn. From turn 2 the prefix up to that turn includes both system blocks plus history, is well over the minimum, and is what is read from cache. The first breakpoint is a bonus when the chart is large enough.

Why does a Western reading get no lordships, aspects or doshas?

factsFor returns undefined for any non-Vedic chart, so buildChartSystem falls back to renderChart. The techniques are Vedic, and whole-sign house numbers would contradict the Placidus houses the PDF and the iOS view draw from chart.planets[].house.

Why is the length rule in the prompt rather than in max_tokens?

Because max_tokens truncates; it does not shorten. A model told to write 160 words writes about 160 and stops cleanly. The ceiling of 1,600 exists so a runaway answer is bounded, not to shape a normal one.

What does selectHistory do with a stored assistant turn that has no user turn before it in the window?

Drops it. The window is trimmed from the front until the first turn is a user turn, because the API requires that and because an answer with no question reads as a non sequitur. The test named "never starts on an assistant turn" pins it, including the case where the result is empty.

What does injection.test.ts guard against?

A data helper that ignores the injected bearer client and builds its own cookie client. On an iOS request that client has no session, RLS denies the write, and the reading is never stored.
